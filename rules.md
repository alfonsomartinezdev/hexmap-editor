# Songs of Creation

## I. Gods

Songs of Creation is a game of building a world together. Each player is a god. The world begins empty, and over three ages the players fill it. Land is cheapest in the first age, peoples in the second, deeds in the third.

Each round every player receives power and spends it on the powers below, or saves it. A player may spend power on any creation on the map (with some exceptions: please read alignment).

## II. The World

Before the first round, agree on three things and write them down.

**The myth.** How the world began, in a few lines. Include its shape: one landmass, many islands, or a sea with coasts.

**The palette.** What belongs in this world and what does not: peoples, magic, technology, tone.

**Lines and veils.** A line is a subject the game does not touch. A veil is a subject that may happen but is not described.

## III. A Round at a Glance

1. **Power.** One player rolls 2d6. Every player receives that much power. A player who ended the last round with 5 or fewer power receives the saving bonus on top.
2. **Edicts.** A player who has not used their edict this age may declare one now, before anyone posts. It changes one thing for everyone, this round only.
3. **Posts.** Each player posts once, in any order. A post spends power on any powers the player likes, on any creation on the map. Unspent power carries over. Costs are in the Table of Powers.
4. **Reactions.** An army marching on a city, an army, or a land is a conflict. Any player may react to it, within their post or in a second post if they have already posted.
5. **Resolution.** Conflicts resolve at the end of the round, after every reaction.
6. **The age.** From the fifth round of an age, the players vote at the end of the round. A majority ends the age; from the tenth round, one vote does.

An area is up to 19 hexes in the first age, up to 7 in the second, and 1 in the third.

## IV. Creation

The game is played in rounds. Each round has three phases, in order: conversation and edicts; posts; then reactions and the resolution of conflicts.

At the start of each round, one player rolls 2d6, and every player receives that much power. 

A player who ends a round with 5 or fewer power gains 1 extra power the next round; 2 the round after if they end low again; then 3, to a maximum of 3. The bonus starts over at the beginning of each age.

A turn is one player's post, in which they spend their power on as many powers as they'd like. Each player makes one post a round, in any order. A player who does not post keeps their unspent power.

An area is the most hexes one use of a power can cover: 19 in the first age, 7 in the second, 1 in the third. A use may cover fewer hexes than that, in any shape. A land form is one connected feature on the map: a range, a forest, or a sea. It may be made of any number of areas.

### Reactions

A conflict is an army marching on something: a city, another army, a land. A player who has already posted may post again to react to a conflict begun by a later post. A player who has not yet posted may react within their post. A reaction spends power like any post and acts only on the conflict it answers. Conflicts resolve at the end of the round, after every reaction.

### Edicts

Once per age, a player may declare an edict at the start of a round, before anyone has posted. It makes one change, for every player, for that round and no longer. An edict can:

- Give every land a nature in addition to its own. This round, Rouse through that nature, Shape Land making a land of it, Shape Climate giving it, and Exalt of a wonder of it each cost 2 less, to a minimum of 1.
- Set the cost of one power for one kind of target (forests, armies, treaties) to its price in another age or lower it by 2, to a minimum of 1.
- Forbid one power or deed.

Edicts declared in the same round all apply. Two edicts that cannot both apply cancel each other, and both are spent. In their place comes a cataclysm: every player who declared neither edict decides together what happens to the world, and writes it before anyone posts that round.

### The First Age

The first age is the age of land. The map begins nearly blank. The players raise mountains, cut rivers, spread forests and seas, give each land its nature, and exalt the first wonders.

A round of the first age is about **500 years**. An area is up to 19 hexes, about 5 across.

### The Second Age

The second age is the age of peoples. Ancestries are raised and settle the land the first age made. Heritages split from them. The first cities are founded.

A round of the second age is about **100 years**. An area is up to 7 hexes, about 3 across.

### The Third Age

The third age is the age of history: cities, armies, and legends.

A round of the third age is **a few years**. An area is a single hex.

### When an Age Ends

Rounds are grouped into three ages. Each age lasts at least five rounds. From the fifth round on, the players vote at the end of each round; if a majority votes to end the age, the next age begins. From the tenth round on, one vote is enough. When the third age ends, the game is over.

When the first age ends, and again when the second ends, each player does two things at no cost before the next age begins:

- Names one place where something happened during the age, and gives it one line of history. The place keeps the name and the line.
- Uses Shape Land up to three times on land that already exists, to expand or reshape it. The area is the size of the age that is ending.

## V. Natures

A land form may have a nature. A nature is a keyword that says what a place is like and what it tends to do. Each nature has an opposite. Natures cost nothing and have no rules of their own. They guide what is plausible there, including what might happen when Event or Catastrophe strikes nearby, and what kind of wonder Exalt might raise. Changing a place's nature uses Shape Climate. Calling on a land to do what its nature says uses Rouse.

| Quickening | Something in this land brings things to life                               |
| Burning    | Something in this land is on fire                                          |
| Frozen     | Something in this land never thaws                                         |
| Carved     | Something in this land looks made                                          |
| Tangled    | Nothing in this land runs straight                                         |
| Deep       | This land goes further down than up                                        |
| Skyward    | This land goes further up than down                                        |
| Faithful   | This land keeps its word and makes good what goes wrong                    |
| Wild       | Nothing in this land has ever been tamed, and no two years of it are alike |
| Fading     | Everything in this land is thin on the ground                              |
| Teeming    | This land is full of living things                                         |
| Hungry     | Something in this land is always taking                                    |
| Lavish     | This land gives more than anything on it could use                         |
| Radiant    | This land is bright                                                        |
| Veiled     | This land is concealed or covered in something                             |
| Roaring    | This land is never quiet                                                   |
| Still      | Nothing in this land moves, and nothing makes a sound                      |

**Baneful.** Something in this land does harm to whatever stays. Ground that shakes once a season, sulphur on the mountain's breath, a green skin on the lake, a meadow on the plain with flowers whose honey kills. A baneful sea might be one filled with feuding leviathans. 

**Quickening.** Something in this land brings things to life. A spring the sick beasts of the world drag themselves toward, a forest where felled trees stand back up, a sea that gives back the drowned breathing. A quickening plain might green the day after a fire. A quickening river might carry seeds that root in bare rock.

**Burning.** Something in this land is on fire. Vents that smoke, springs that boil, ground warm enough to melt snow in winter. A burning plain might burn to black every summer and come back green. A burning lake might have flames standing on the water some nights. A burning mountain might have fire in its caves and smoke on its slopes.

**Frozen.** Something in this land never thaws. Glaciers grinding down the valleys, snow lying in midsummer, trees locked in rime. A frozen sea might be walked across. A frozen lake might keep whole what fell into it a thousand years ago.

**Carved.** Something in this land looks made. Arches and pillars of stone, terraces, a canyon with faces in its walls. A carved coast might be a line of columns marching into the sea. A carved river might run in a too-straight channel.

**Tangled.** Nothing in this land runs straight. Vines grown through vines, a river braided into a hundred channels that split and rejoin, ridges folded so the pass comes back to where it began. A tangled coast might be a maze of inlets longer than the land it bounds. A tangled swamp might have channels that loop back on themselves however you steer. A tangled hedge might have a way through it that no one has followed to the end.

**Deep.** This land goes further down than up. Caves that run under everything, wells that find no bottom, roots that go further than the trees are tall. A deep lake might never have been sounded. A deep forest might be old growth with something older under it. Things come up from below.

**Skyward.** This land goes further up than down. Peaks above the clouds, thin air, birds by the million. A skyward forest might have trees whose tops no one has seen. A skyward plateau might be closer to the stars than to the sea. Things fall from the sky here.

**Faithful.** This land keeps its word and makes good what goes wrong. The river floods to the same stone every spring and has never missed, the pass opens the week the snow has always gone, a crop planted late still comes in. A faithful valley might hold its warmth all winter.

**Wild.** Nothing in this land has ever been tamed, and no two years of it are alike. Beasts unafraid, seasons that come when they like. A wild forest might breed things no one has named. A wild coast might have no two tides alike. A wild plain might be the kind that swallows roads and expeditions.

**Fading.** Everything in this land is thin on the ground. One tree where there was a wood, a spring that gives a cupful, herds you count on one hand, colors that have gone grey. A fading forest might be more clearing than trees. A fading sea might be shallow enough to wade for a mile. A fading mountain might be worn to a long low hill, with the roots of the old peak showing through.

**Teeming.** This land is full of living things. Salmon runs you could walk across, herds to the horizon, cliffs white with nesting birds, swamps so loud with frogs in spring that no one sleeps. A teeming meadow might be grazed to dirt and green again in a week. A teeming river might fill every net in the valley one year and every field with locusts the next. A teeming forest might have more eyes in it than leaves.

**Hungry.** Something in this land is always taking. Wolves no cull thins, winters that take the old of every herd, a sea that loses a boat a season whatever the pilot. A hungry forest might be one that beasts walk into and don't walk out of. A hungry bog might keep everything it has ever swallowed. It wants nothing in particular; it simply costs to be here.

**Lavish.** This land gives more than anything on it could use. Fish so thick the river shakes, herds that shake the ground, gold in the gravel of every stream. A lavish valley might bear three harvests a year. A lavish mountain might be honeycombed with veins of every metal. 

**Radiant.** This land is bright. Forests glow with luminescent fungus, cliffs shine white from the sea, and the winter sky burns with color. A volcano's light is seen across the land. A radiant lake might be filled with schools of glowing fish. A radiant desert is blinding from dawn to dusk.

**Veiled.** This land is concealed or covered in something. Mist that never lifts, shade that reaches the ground at noon, nights that run long. A veiled valley might be filled with fog most of the year and drawn wrong on every map. A veiled forest might be dark under the canopy, with pale-eyed things that hunt by ear. A veiled sea might be black a hand's depth down.

**Roaring.** This land is never quiet. Surf that shakes the cliffs, rapids heard a valley away, a wind that has not dropped in living memory, thunder walking the ridges at night. A roaring coast might be a coast of wrecks. A roaring forest might be one where the canopy sounds like the sea. A roaring plain might be a place where you watch the weather coming for three days and hear it for two.

**Still.** Nothing in this land moves, and nothing makes a sound. No wind, no current, no tree falling, no bird. A still sea might be glass to the horizon. A still bog might keep what it takes exactly as it took it. A still valley might be one where sound carries for miles because nothing else is making any.

## VI. Power

Every turn, a player may spend power on any of the powers below, or save it. Nothing limits how much a player does in a turn except the power they have.

The powers are grouped by what they act on: land, peoples and cities, legends, and fate. Powers that work on one area use the area of the current age, given under Creation.

### Land

Shape and reshape the world itself, and call on it to act.

| Power | First age | Second age | Third age |
| --------------------- | :------------: | :------------------: | :-----------------: |
| Shape Land, 1 area | 3 | 5 | 8 |
| Shape Climate, 1 area | 2 | 4 | 6 |
| Rouse, 1 area | 4 | 4 | 4 |

#### Shape Land

Raise, reshape, or wear away the land: mountains, hills, rivers, lakes, forests, jungles, deserts, grassland, tundra, coast, and sea. One use covers up to one area's worth of hexes, in any shape: a line of hexes for a river or a range, a cluster for a forest, a ring for a valley. Used again and again, it makes ranges, inland seas, and wastes. A river may run through hexes that already hold other land, such as a mountain or a forest. Both land forms keep those hexes.

#### Shape Climate

Change the weather, seasons, or nature within one area. The hexes themselves do not change.
#### Rouse

Through this power, a player calls on the land within one area to act on its nature. Whatever it does lasts until the end of the round. Nothing is destroyed and nothing is changed permanently. What happens must follow from the land's nature.
### Peoples and Cities

Bring ancestries and heritages into the world.

| Power | First age | Second age | Third age |
| --------------- | :------------: | :------------------: | :-----------------: |
| Create Ancestry | 22 | 6 | 15 |
| Create Heritage | 12 | 4 | 10 |

#### Create Ancestry

Bring a people into the world. An ancestry may be any kind of people. Each ancestry begins settled in an area and lives with natures of its land from the start. Players may pool power to create an ancestry within a round; if it is not created by the round's end, the power returns to its owners. A legend arises within the new people for each player who paid toward it, aligned toward that player at the first level.

#### Create Heritage

Split a people from a people. A heritage shares an ancestor with an existing ancestry and has become its own, by where it lives, how it lives, or what happened to it.

### Legends

Act through the figures and companies the world remembers. The powers below the divider are deeds. A people performs a deed only through a legend present where it happens.

| Power              | First age | Second age | Third age |
| ------------------ | :-------: | :--------: | :-------: |
| Create Legend      |    10     |     7      |     8     |
| Resurrect Legend | 5 | 5 | 5 |
| Perform Deed*      |     2     |     1      |     1     |
| **Deeds** | | | |
| Found City, 1 hex  |     8     |     4      |     3     |
| Settle, 1 area     |     8     |     4      |     3     |
| Build              |     6     |     4      |     2     |
| Raise Army         |     6     |     4      |     2     |
| March Army         |     4     |     3      |     2     |
| Treat              |     4     |     3      |     2     |

\* Once per round; the legend must be exalted.

#### Create Legend

Bring a legend into the world: a name the peoples will remember. A legend may be one figure, a dynasty, or a company of many, a guild, a sect, a crew, an order of knights, so long as the world remembers it as one thing.

A legend acts as one character. It has one name. It may be anywhere its kind could plausibly reach, may pass from one people to another, and may exist before any people does.

A legend gives presence: a people acts through it, by the deeds in the table above. Exalting a legend gives it more; see Exalt.

#### Resurrect Legend

Bring back a legend that was killed or destroyed. Only the player the legend is aligned toward may.

#### Perform Deed

Once a round, an exalted legend can perform one deed for this cost instead of the deed's own. Any other deed that round costs what the table says.

An exalted legend may also use Perform Deed, once in its life, to create a heritage. A legend exalted twice is a mythical progenitor: it may use Perform Deed to create an ancestry instead, once. An ancestry founded this way does not come with an aligned legend.

#### Found City

A people founds a city: one hex, in a land form they live in, with a name. A city is a place on the map that can Build and Raise Army, and that other powers can act on.

#### Settle

A people spreads into one area next to where they already live. From then on they live there too, with whatever nature the land has. Two peoples may live in the same land.

#### Build

A city builds one thing. What it has built counts in battle and in what the city is known for. Build cannot make a wonder; that is Exalt.
#### Raise Army

A city raises one army, and no city raises more than one a round. An army stands where it was raised until it is moved, and lasts until it is destroyed, disbanded, or folded into another. Moving and fighting are under Conflict.

#### March Army

An army moves, or goes against a city or another army. Where it goes and what it meets is written in the post; what happens when it arrives is resolved under Conflict, at the end of the round.

#### Treat

Two parties make a treaty: two cities, two settlements, or two peoples. The legend may be present with either party. While it holds, the armies of each party may not march on the other. The scope is set when the treaty is made, from one city and one settlement up to two peoples entire. A treaty may be broken at any time, but breaking it costs the same as making it, paid before the army marches.
### Fate

Raise the world's creations above their kind, turn its fates, and draw them to your side.

|Power| First age | Second age | Third age |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Exalt | 6 | 5 | 6 |
| Align | 5 | 5 | 5 |
|Event|10|7|9|
|Catastrophe, 1 area|10|10|10|

#### Exalt

Exalt gives one thing no other of its kind has. A land form, a people, or a legend may be exalted. A land form may be exalted once, resulting in a wonder. A people may be exalted once per field. A legend may be exalted twice.

**A land form** gains a wonder: one thing in it, or the whole of it becoming its nature entirely. The land's nature says what fits.

**A people** gains one field: a science, craft, or magic. While the people persists, no other people equals them in it.

**A legend** gains something it carries, or something it can do, that becomes its story. An exalted legend can Perform Deed; see Perform Deed for what a legend exalted once or twice may found.

#### Align

Align draws a creation toward a player. A land form, a people, a city, or a legend can be aligned toward a single player, at one of two levels. Everything starts unaligned, and anyone may act on an unaligned thing at the normal cost.

Each use of Align moves the target one step toward the player who uses it. If the target is aligned toward another player, Align lowers that alignment by one level. If it is unaligned or already aligned toward you, Align raises it by one level. A player may align any one target only once per turn. Align itself is never made dearer or blocked by alignment, and a player who aligns a target down may act on it in the same post.

At the second level, only the aligned player may act on the target; another player must first align it down to the first level. A player may hold any number of legends aligned toward them at the first level. Second-level alignments are capped, whatever the target: a player may hold 1 in the first age, 2 in the second, 3 in the third.

|Alignment|Cost for other players|
|---|---|
|First level|Double the power's normal cost|
|Second level|The aligned player only|

#### Event

Something changes permanently. A legend escapes death. A craft is lost, or found. A succession fails. Two peoples are joined. A dragon's essence shifts from frost to flame. Event can happen to anything, anywhere, and is not bound to a land's nature. It does not destroy.
#### Catastrophe

Something is destroyed: a city, an army, a legend, a people's exalted art, or the land within one area. Catastrophe is the only power that destroys. To erase something completely, the wipe-out cost under Conflict is paid as well. Ruin comes by whatever means the player chooses; natures say what shape it takes.
## VII. Conflict

Conflicts resolve at the end of the round. The players involved write the outcome. If any of them objects, roll.

1. For each exalted field the army's people could use in battle, the army gets +1. Higher ground, walls, and favorable terrain or weather are each worth +1, to a maximum of +3 in all.
2. Each army rolls 2d6 and adds its bonuses. The higher total wins; the loser is broken. On a tie the battle is a stalemate and continues next round, unless one side is marched away.
3. An army marching on a city with no army takes it.

A broken army can no longer act, and a new army must be raised in its place. It is not wiped out unless the wipe-out cost is paid.

When several armies meet, roll once for each battle. An army that fights more than one battle in a round tires: −1 to its roll for each battle after the first.

### Shared Land

Two players shaping the same land in one round is not a conflict; no one reacts, and nothing is rolled. The later post stands, but it does not erase the earlier one: something of the first shaping remains in the land and the two players decide between them what.

### Wiping Out

Nothing is destroyed completely by default; survivors remain. To destroy something completely, an army, a legend, a city, a heritage, an ancestry, the player pays what it would cost to create in the current age, on top of whatever power destroyed it. Land destroyed by Catastrophe becomes bare land with no nature. A legend destroyed completely can still be resurrected.

## VIII. Archetypes

Before the first round, each player chooses an archetype: one of the nine primordial forces of creation below. Two players may choose the same one. The archetype is who you are for the whole game: the thing that was there before the land, and made it. If none of the nine is the force you want to be, make your own. Give it two natures, a motto, and six prompts, and it belongs at the table like any of these.

An archetype does not limit what a player may do. An archetype is who you are, not what you may do.

If you have power to spend and nothing comes to mind, take a prompt from your archetype. Do what it says with part of your turn and spend the rest as you like, or take another. A prompt that cannot be done on the current map is skipped.

### The Heartwood

*The word for world is forest.*

**Natures.** Faithful, Quickening.

**Who you are.** The seasons that keep their word. Leaves that turn the same week every year, a river that floods to the same stone every spring, a wood cut to the stump that stands again by the seventh year. Everything in your lands has its place and its time, and what is taken comes back.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for forests and the rivers that run through them.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Faithful or Quickening.
- Rouse, so a river floods on its day or a cut forest stands again.

**Prompts.**
- Raise a forest in the middle of the largest landmass.
- Lay a river from the nearest mountains through a forest to the sea; if there are no mountains, start it at the highest land.
- Give one of your Natures to the longest river on the map.
- Exalt a tree in a forest. It might be the tree that holds up the sky, a tree whose roots are the roads of the underworld, a tree that speaks, etc.
- Create a legend who arose from a forest.
- Treat between two peoples who share a river.

### The Long Night

*There was never really nothing.*

**Natures.** Veiled, Still.

**Who you are.** The dark and the quiet that were here before the light. Mist that never lifts, a valley drawn wrong on every map, a sea black a hand's depth down and flat as glass. You cover what others make bright and quiet what they make loud, and what you keep in the dark stays as it was.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for valleys and inland seas.
- Shape Climate, to make a bright land Veiled or a loud land Still.
- Rouse, so the fog takes a road or the wind dies on a fleet.

**Prompts.**
- Lay an inland sea in the largest empty lowland.
- Give your nature to the smallest terrain feature on the map.
- Exalt a valley or a sea. It might be a valley of permanent night, a sea that has never had a wave, a sea whose surface is solid glass, etc.
- Create a people who find their way by sound.
- Align a Still land.
- Exalt a single light in a Veiled land: the one star visible from inside the dark, or a fire that has never gone out.

### The Tempest

*Nothing happens twice.*

**Natures.** Wild, Roaring.

**Who you are.** The weather. Surf that shakes the cliffs, a wind that has not dropped in living memory, no two tides alike and no road that holds a season. You go where you are not expected.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for coasts, cliffs, and hills.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Wild or Roaring.
- Rouse, so a storm sits on a city or a flood takes a plain.

**Prompts.**
- Lay a gorge where the longest river leaves the highest land.
- Raise sea cliffs along the coast that faces the open ocean, or lay a line of islands off it.
- Give one of your Natures to a stretch of open sea.
- Exalt a plain, a coast, or a sea. It might be a storm that has stood in one place since the world began, a herd that has never stopped running, a wind that carries voices, etc.
- Create a legend who walked out of a storm.
- Create a legend in a Still or Faithful land as a sign of contention

### The Black Stair

*Shape is what is left after the heat.*

**Natures.** Burning, Carved.

**Who you are.** The fire and what it leaves behind. Volcanoes whose flows cool into columns and terraces, a coast of black pillars marching into the sea, a canyon with faces in its walls. You fire soft ground until it holds a shape.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for volcanoes, canyons, and coasts of stone.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Burning or Carved.
- Rouse, so a volcano erupts.

**Prompts.**
- Raise a volcano within a mountain range or on an island.
- Give Carved to part of a land another player made.
- Exalt a volcano or a stone column. It might be a forge inside the volcano, a stair of black stone into the clouds, a pillar that holds up the sky, etc.
- Create a people who live inside carved stone.
- Create a legend forged in a volcano.
- Build something in black stone that can be seen from the sea.

### The Winter

*What the cold takes, it keeps.*

**Natures.** Frozen, Hungry.

**Who you are.** The cold that takes. Glaciers grinding down the valleys, winters that take the old of every herd, a sea that loses a boat a season, and under the ice everything you have ever taken, whole. You come early when you like.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for mountains and glaciers.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Frozen or Hungry.
- Rouse, so a harbor freezes or the wolves come down.

**Prompts.**
- Lay a glacier down a valley or a mountain.
- Give Frozen to an area that borders a Frozen land.
- Give Hungry to part of a large land form, or along its edge.
- Align something on a Hungry land.
- Exalt a glacier or a frozen lake. It might be a glacier that is a sleeping beast, a lake that holds the first winter, a palace of ice, etc.
- Create a people who follow the edge of the ice.

### The Serpent

*Everything is falling, and everything is mine.*

**Natures.** Deep, Baneful.

**Who you are.** Underneath, and angry. Caves that run under everything, a lake no line has sounded, ground that shakes once a season, sulphur on the mountain's breath. You open the ground under what stands above, and what comes up from you does harm.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for lakes, caves, and low ground.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Deep or Baneful.
- Rouse, so the ground opens under a camp or the gas comes up out of a lake.

**Prompts.**
- Lay caves under a forest or a grassland.
- Give Baneful to part of a land another people hold.
- Exalt a lake or a cave. It might open into the underworld, hold a drowned city, have the serpent itself asleep at the bottom, etc.
- Create a people who live below the ground.
- Create a legend that came up from the deep.
- Settle a people into the caves under another people's city.

### The First Light

*Out of nothing, brilliance.*

**Natures.** Radiant, Lavish.

**Who you are.** The shining and the plenty. Cliffs white from the sea, a winter sky that burns with color, gold in the gravel of every stream, three harvests a year. You light what others hid and give more than anyone can carry away.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for white cliffs and rich valleys.
- Shape Climate, to make a dark land Radiant or a poor land Lavish.
- Rouse, so a land lights the night or a valley gives its harvest early.

**Prompts.**
- Lay a valley from a mountain range to the sea.
- Give Radiant to part of a Veiled land.
- Give Lavish to a stretch of a river, a plain, or a mountain.
- Exalt a peak or a valley. It might be a second sun at night, a stone that fell from the sky, a valley where every stone is gold, etc.
- Create a gilded or beautiful people.
- Command a people to build something in your honor.

### The Flock

*Nothing here is ever alone.*

**Natures.** Skyward, Teeming.

**Who you are.** The height and the crowd. Peaks above the clouds, cliffs white with nesting birds, herds to the horizon, a sky dark with wings at dusk. You fill the high places and leave nothing alone.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for peaks, plateaus, and sea cliffs.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Skyward or Teeming.
- Rouse, so the flock takes a field or the herd comes down the mountain.

**Prompts.**
- Raise a plateau beside a mountain range.
- Give Skyward to part of a plain or a sea.
- Give Teeming to a Skyward land.
- Exalt a peak or a cliff. It might be a roost of birds large enough to ride, a herd that walks on cloud, a tree that holds every bird in the world, etc.
- Create a numerous or far-sighted people.
- Create a legend raised by a flock.

### The Briar

*What grows on thin ground grows thorns.*

**Natures.** Fading, Tangled.

**Who you are.** The thorn on thin ground. A river braided into a hundred channels and most of them dry, a wood that is more thicket than trees, a pass that comes back to where it began. You grow across whatever line was drawn and leave one way through, which you do not mark.

**You reach for.**
- Shape Land, for thickets across open ground and rivers that braid.
- Shape Climate, to make a land Fading or Tangled.
- Rouse, so the thorn takes a road or the channels shift.

**Prompts.**
- Raise a thicket across a plain between two peoples.
- Give Tangled to the land between two peoples.
- Give Fading to a Tangled land.
- Exalt a thicket or a Tangled land. It might be a maze with a garden at its center, a hedge that moves at night, a thorn that grows through stone, etc.
- Create an elusive or rootless people.
- Settle a people into a land another people already hold.
